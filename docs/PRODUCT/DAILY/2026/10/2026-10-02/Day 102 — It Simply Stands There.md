# Day 102 — It Simply Stands There

Stay home.

Oksi has the dentist.

Jr's morning school quest still belongs to the Warden.

So Owling endurance begins a day early.

The Warden notices something.

Oksi always says Owling sleeps perfectly with her during the day when he is at work.

With the Warden, daytime sleep is almost impossible.

There seems to be one reliable method.

Total exhaustion.

Today, eventually, the method works.

Oksi has five teeth extracted.

She drives home.

The Warden walks Owling.

By the time everyone is back, Owling has finally exhausted himself enough to accept sleep.

The Warden does not argue.

He sleeps too.

Stronghold begins a couple of hours earlier.

Which also means a longer drive.

By the time the Warden arrives, he is already waiting for the weekend to become difficult.

A dangerous way to begin a campaign.

He is somewhat rough with Jr.

Oksi responds immediately.

Then the correction becomes the familiar Stronghold argument.

The Warden misbehaves here.

Oksi loves this place from the deep of her heart.

Voices.

Positions.

A pattern both of them know.

This too is part of Stronghold.

Not the part visible in photographs.

Not the bright-green kitchen.

Not the new chairs.

Not the garden.

But part of the place nonetheless.

Eventually there is furniture.

Six chairs arrived.

The Warden assembles one.

Then another.

Then another.

Then another.

Four of six.

The table watches untouched.

Zero of one.

The Warden falls asleep before the campaign can advance further.

Outside, though, another campaign needs nothing.

The fence stands.

For weeks it had been a problem.

A decision.

A price.

A contract.

A schedule.

Then installers.

Then construction.

Now:

Nothing.

No action required.

No one to call.

No choice to make.

No hole to dig.

The fence simply stands in its place.

Perhaps this is how Stronghold changes.

Not when a campaign is completed.

But on the first day afterward.

When something that consumed attention stops asking for any.

Four chairs inside.

Two still waiting.

A table still flat.

The family tired.

The weekend barely begun.

And around it all, quietly,

the fence simply stands there.
