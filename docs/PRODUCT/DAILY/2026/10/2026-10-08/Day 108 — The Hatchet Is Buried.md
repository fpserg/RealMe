# Day 108 — The Hatchet Is Buried

Another morning arrives.

Owling wakes around seven.

Closer to normal.

Office again.

The Platform speech.

The rehearsal.

Oksi's angry messages continue, but then one changes the day's shape.

Owling is ill.

Come home.

Immediately.

Or at least before the trip to School 57.

The Warden has a problem.

Today is the rehearsal.

Tomorrow he must be home while Oksi visits the dentist.

There is no obvious free slot.

He accelerates.

He and Alexander speak before Andrey.

Thirty minutes allotted.

One hour used.

The Warden's part is largely all right.

Alexander's smaller section takes too much time.

They make corrections.

Then the Warden runs home.

No replies from Oksi.

He does not know which version of the afternoon is waiting.

Will he take Owling?

Has Oksi taken him along?

Has Jr missed School 57?

Home.

Nobody there.

The answer is already on the road.

Maksim comes back from school.

The Warden gets him food.

Then Oksi returns.

The Warden runs outside.

Helps carry Owling in.

He feels the fever.

Poor baby.

Then another journey.

Pick up Jr from School 57.

The bright boy has managed his homework.

And learned his verses by heart.

Home again.

Oksi has Spanish.

The Warden stays with Owling.

The child is no longer the little engine of the previous days.

Weak.

Silent.

Falling asleep in his father's arms.

Oksi finishes her lesson.

Medicine.

The fever does not go away.

Then she sees red dots on his feet.

Call an ambulance.

The Warden thinks it may be too much.

He calls anyway.

The ambulance arrives.

By then Owling seems much better.

The doctor examines him.

Leaves.

No diagnosis enters the Chronicle.

The parents sit beside their child.

He is energetic again.

And the Warden notices something else.

Warmth.

Between him and Oksi.

The warmth that had seemed absent for the whole week.

No speech announces it.

No argument settles every grievance.

They are simply there together.

Beside Owling.

When the child finally sleeps, they have a normal evening as a couple.

The Warden knows the illness is not over merely because Owling improved.

But the cold war, in his account, is.

Yesterday the flowers had opened the possibility.

Today the Warden gives it a name.

The hatchet is buried.

And another connection reopened.

Pasha.

An old friend from Newcastle.

Moscow, Kiev, and meetings elsewhere.

Then the war, and silence.

The Warden had feared that friendship might no longer survive.

But Pasha wrote warmly from a life now based in Warsaw with his wife, while vacationing in Portugal.

An invitation remained.

If you're around, come by.

A connection repaired across years and borders.
