# Day 100 — The Wasteland Is a Stadium

Another busy day.

Two calls.

Then an asset manager in person.

Inna prepared for him as though the 2000s had never entirely ended.

Tea.

Fresh raspberry.

Fine porcelain cups.

The whole set.

The guest himself was a simple modern guy.

Probably indifferent to the ceremony.

But Inna had worked for Russian oligarchs.

This was her grammar of importance.

And perhaps importance was not entirely imaginary.

The asset manager might have a voice when best-analyst votes were counted.

Maybe every raspberry counted.

Then the side task stopped being a side task.

Platform Economy.

The report had lived under the shadow of Strategy deadlines.

Something to get done.

Something the juniors and AI had carried a large part of.

Publish it.

Move on.

Except bank management had noticed.

They wanted a live presentation.

Next week.

Andrey was excited.

The Warden was genuinely surprised.

Then the surprise acquired weight.

A published report could disappear into the archive.

A live presentation could not.

Now the argument belonged to him again.

Not merely as the name above the team.

As the person who would have to stand there and make it hold.

Recognition and responsibility arrived in the same envelope.

Then evening changed the scale.

Pick up Jr from football.

Walk Owling.

The stadium was ordinary enough.

Jr trained there.

Children ran across the field.

Except the Warden knew what had been underneath it.

A wasteland.

His wasteland.

The place where he had played football with Sanya as a child.

And beside it stood the apartment block where Sanya had lived then.

The funeral was over.

There was no coffin here.

No need to say anything.

Just geography.

The boy the Warden had once been had played football with Sanya on this ground.

Now the Warden's son played football here.

The wasteland was a stadium.

One childhood had not replaced the other.

It had simply been built on top of it.

Then home.

The Battle by the Cradle.

Owling refused to surrender quickly.

Minutes disappeared.

Then more.

Oksi had hoped for a nighttime Stronghold quest.

The available window closed.

The Warden could be sad about that.

He was, apparently.

Privately, however, gratitude.

He was tired.

The night venture could wait.

Owling had provided an impeccable excuse.

Day 100 had begun with work that refused to stay finished.

It ended with a Stronghold quest that never began.

And between them stood a football field.

Where the Warden once ran with Sanya.

Where Jr runs now.

The wasteland is a stadium.
