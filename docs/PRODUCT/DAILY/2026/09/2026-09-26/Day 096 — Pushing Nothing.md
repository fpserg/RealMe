# Day 096 — Pushing Nothing

Owling allowed the Household a graceful beginning.

07:30.

Almost generous.

Supplies.

A walk.

A little gardening while he slept.

Then it was time to go.

Sanya's funeral.

Sergey Jr wanted to come.

The Warden's first instinct was no.

Oksi thought differently.

Jr could help with Owling.

A practical reason.

Enough.

They set off.

Near the funeral location, the Warden saw two teenagers kicking bags filled with leaves.

He smiled.

Sadly.

Of course.

That was exactly the sort of thing he and Sanya would have done twenty-five years ago.

No purpose.

No achievement.

Just two boys discovering that bags of leaves could be kicked.

Maybe they would still have done it now.

If Sanya were around.

Then came the people.

People the Warden had not seen for ages.

And among them, Roma.

Sanya's older son.

Fourteen years old.

As tall as the Warden.

The last time the Warden had seen him, Roma had been Owling's age.

There are many ways to measure a long absence.

A child becoming a young man is one of them.

Jr found his place at the funeral.

He ran with Owling.

Kept him entertained.

Made himself useful.

Oksi had been right.

The Warden and Oksi had a little more room to be there.

Then came the farewell.

The Warden approached the coffin.

Looked at Sanya's face.

He had expected more change.

There was less.

Enough of the old Sanya remained visible.

The companion for crazy things.

The boy who would kick bags of leaves.

The friend from the photographs.

The man from whom the Warden had walked away.

There had been years in which something might still have been said.

Then suddenly there were none.

So the Warden said it now.

Quietly.

I forgive you.

Forgive me too.

No argument.

No accounting.

No attempt to decide which of them had carried more blame through the intervening years.

Just the words.

Then Sanya's hair.

That familiar hay hair.

The Warden touched it briefly.

Sergey Jr was approaching.

The Warden left.

That was enough for a child to see.

The rest belonged to two old friends.

Then Stronghold.

Life goes on.

The children held a late-night movie show in Maksim's room.

Alisa and Jr needed an intervention.

Brief.

Resolved.

Owling needed sleep.

That was less brief.

The Warden brawled with him until eventually the smaller combatant surrendered.

Victory.

At the price of complete exhaustion.

And still there were things to remove.

Carton boxes left behind by the Kitchen and Basement campaigns.

The Warden drove them away.

Fifteen minutes in the car.

Alone.

After the funeral, it felt like meditation.

Then there was the bush.

Too large for the car.

Of course.

The wheelbarrow came out.

The bush went in.

Another quest.

Beyond the borders of Neroschino.

Dispose.

Turn around.

Now the wheelbarrow was empty.

The Warden began walking back.

The moon was full.

A day earlier, he and Oksi had sat on the Stronghold deck with warm tea and four sleeping children and silently agreed that they had done everything right.

Today he had gone back into the life that existed before all of that.

He had seen Roma grown.

He had seen Sanya's face.

He had said the words that would never receive an answer.

He had touched the old hay hair.

Then he had returned home.

Children.

Arguments.

Bedtime.

Boxes.

Bushes.

The ordinary maintenance of a life still happening.

Behind him lay twenty-five years that could no longer be rearranged.

Ahead was Stronghold.

The wheelbarrow rolled beneath his hands.

Empty.

Under the full moon, the Warden walked home.

Pushing nothing.
