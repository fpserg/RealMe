# Day 075 — The Smell of Autumn

The day began before the morning really existed.

Owling woke at five. Rain covered everything outside, and the second attempt at sleep lasted only briefly. There was a book instead — something he has been insisting on for days — while the Warden was already beginning to feel a cold settling in.

The rain became one of the day's governing forces. Owling had energy with nowhere obvious to put it. There was a broken clay pot before Oksi woke, a short successful nap later, and then another awakening into yelling and rampaging. Going outside, normally the simplest answer, repeatedly became impossible or temporary.

Oksi continued her garden campaign. She returned with more things for Stronghold, and later there were heavy bags of soil to move. Twice during the day the Warden and Oksi collided at moments when each needed something immediately: he needed relief while trying to contain Owling; she needed help with the physical work around the garden. The pressure became personal.

There was still Alisa, briefly a Martian who could not speak, before another burst of rain sent everyone rushing home.

At the hardest point, the Warden felt intense frustration with Stronghold and took Owling outside again.

Then something changed without anything really changing.

Owling fell asleep in the stroller. The rain had left wet grass and leaves. Somewhere farther away there was the smell of smoke, perhaps burning leaves. The Warden walked through puddles in rubber boots and suddenly the landscape belonged not only to Stronghold but to another place and another time: his parents' country house, when he himself was a small child, only a little older than Owling is now.

There was nothing remarkable about the scene. That was precisely why the memory arrived so naturally. Wet ground, autumn air, smoke, boots. For a few minutes the day stopped demanding anything, and the Warden felt better.

Even the fuel quest, anticipated mainly because it promised rare solitude, was unexpectedly easy. There was almost no queue. Oksi asked for medicine and water, so the trip stretched farther in search of a pharmacy. Instead of becoming another burden, the extra distance helped the Warden recover.

When he returned, all the children were already in bed.

The evening with Oksi was warm. She talked about Stronghold differently from how the Warden had experienced it during the hardest hours. She loved this place. She loved her garden. She loved what was being made here.

And so the day ended far from its hardest moment, but without cancelling it.

Oksi loved Stronghold. The Warden had struggled with it. Between those two truths stood the smell of an autumn childhood unexpectedly rediscovered while pushing Owling through puddles.

Perhaps Stronghold does not have to become easy before it can become beloved.

The Warden left himself a note instead: he has to learn to love this place despite all the hardships he faces here.
