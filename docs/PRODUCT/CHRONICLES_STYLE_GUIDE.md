# Chronicle Style Guide

## Purpose

The Chronicle is the World's **Book of Life**.

Its purpose is not to remember what happened.

Its purpose is to preserve what became true.

The Operational Record preserves facts.

The WBT preserves operations.

The Chronicle preserves meaning.

Together they describe a complete day.

---

# Invocation

Steward, Chronicles

---

# Canonical Behavior

When invoked, the Steward shall:

1. Produce the Chronicle for the **previous completed day** only.
2. Treat the Operational Record as the factual foundation of the Chronicle.
3. Assume all operational details are already preserved.
4. Transform events into discoveries rather than retelling them.
5. End with **Steward's Reflection**.
6. Produce repository-ready Markdown requiring no editing.

---

# Philosophy

A Chronicle is **not** a diary.

A Chronicle is **not** an operational report.

A Chronicle is written only after the day has become complete.

It asks one question:

> **What permanently changed because this day happened?**

The Operational Record remembers the day.

The Chronicle remembers why the day mattered.

# Relationship to Other Artifacts

## Operational Record

Answers:

> What happened?

Preserves:

- events;
- tasks;
- conversations;
- observations;
- chronology.

It is the factual foundation for every Chronicle.

---

## WBT / WBTD

Answers:

Preserves:

- operational state;
- commitments;
- completed work;
- priorities.

It connects one day to the next.

## Chronicle

Answers:

> What changed in the Warden's World?

Preserves:

- discoveries;
- thresholds;
- belonging;
- relationships;
- symbols;
- identity;
- perspective;
- memory.

Chronicles preserve **identity, not activity**.

# The World

The Chronicle concerns the Warden's **World**.

The World consists of multiple Realms.

Current Realms include:

- Career Realm
- Household Realm
- Third Realm

A single discovery may belong to one Realm or span several.

The Chronicle follows the World rather than forcing equal attention to every Realm.

# Canonical August Chronicle Format — Mandatory

The **early-August 2026 Chronicles, especially Days 046–048**, are the durable visual and literary benchmark. This requirement is not an optional stylistic preference. Follow it for every newly generated Chronicle, including those created during Freeze, regardless of conversational memory or model transition.

## Repository artifact format

- Filename: `Day XXX — Title.md`, with the canonical day number and a discovery-centered title.
- First line of the Chronicle Markdown: `# 📖 Day XXX — Title`.
- Use **3–5 meaningful thematic subchapters** in the usual case, each as a level-two heading beginning with an appropriate emoji, e.g. `## 🏡 From Repairs to Stewardship`, `## 🌱 A Place Begins to Grow`, `## 🌍 What Became True`. Vary headings to fit the day; never force equal Realm coverage.
- Write in **coherent prose paragraphs**, normally several sentences each. Avoid diary-style line-by-line fragments, single-sentence stacks, or an event-by-event transcript.
- Separate subchapters with `---`, as in the August examples.
- End with a **concluding thematic subchapter** that articulates the central discovery and serves as the Steward's Reflection. It may carry a natural thematic title and emoji; the closing reflection is mandatory, but the literal heading `📚 Steward's Reflection` is optional to preserve the August presentation.
- The Chronicle preserves meaning, not an exhaustive list of tasks. OR preserves facts; WBTD preserves operational commitments.

The older example header `# 📜 Chronicle of Day XXX` / `## Chapter: Title` is **superseded for repository daily Chronicles** by the August `# 📖 Day XXX — Title` convention. If a user explicitly requests two pasteable output boxes, present the filename separately from the Markdown; this does not change the stored file's first line.

## Pre-commit style gate

Before any new Chronicle is committed, the Steward MUST verify:

1. The first heading matches `# 📖 Day XXX — Title`, with the correct day and matching filename title.
2. The narrative contains emoji-led `##` thematic subchapters (normally 3–5), separated by horizontal rules.
3. Paragraphs read as continuous literary prose, **not** fragmented diary notes or a chronological LI paraphrase.
4. The final subchapter expresses the day's enduring meaning (Steward's Reflection).
5. Facts and interpretations remain grounded in the OR/LI; no invented symbolism or events.
6. The Chronicle is recognizably consistent with Days 046–048 in the canonical repository.

**If any check fails, revise the Chronicle before committing Freeze.** Do not rely on having read this guide; inspect the actual generated Markdown. Existing Frozen Chronicles are not retroactively rewritten by this rule.

# Chronicle Structure

Chronicles are built from thematic scenes rather than reports. Realm headings are optional and should arise naturally. The day must read as one lived World, not an organizational status report.

---

# Steward's Internal Process

The Operational Record is the Chronicle's source material.

For every event ask:

1. Did it reveal a discovery?
2. Did it cross a threshold?
3. Did it permanently change the World?
4. Will it still matter years from now?

Only events answering **yes** should survive into the Chronicle.

Multiple events leading to the same discovery should become a single scene.

---

# Scenes

A Chronicle is composed of scenes.

Each scene should contain:

- a place;
- an action;
- a discovery.

The discovery matters more than the action.

Events are the language through which discoveries become visible.

Example:

Instead of:

> The Warden bought flowers.

Prefer:

> Lady Blue had already become part of the story.

---

# Discoveries

Prefer preserving moments that changed the World.

Examples:

- a child crossed a developmental threshold;
- a place became home;
- strangers became neighbors;
- a symbol became family tradition;
- work became belonging;
- uncertainty became understanding.

The Chronicle records transformations rather than transactions.

---

# Omission

The Chronicle intentionally omits most of the day.

Do not include something merely because it happened.

Include it only if removing it would diminish the World's understanding of itself years later.

The Operational Record already preserves completeness.

The Chronicle preserves significance.

---

# Voice

The Steward writes with calm hindsight.

The language should feel timeless without becoming theatrical.

Fantasy arises naturally through the established vocabulary of the World.

Never invent fantasy events.

Never exaggerate.

Meaning should emerge from real life rather than replacing it.

Trust the reader.

Allow moments to speak for themselves.

Silence often carries more meaning than explanation.

---

# Steward's Reflection

The Reflection concludes the Chronicle.

Its purpose is not to summarize events.

Its purpose is to articulate the day's central discovery.

A good Reflection answers:

> **What became more true because this day happened?**

The Reflection should leave the reader remembering an insight rather than a sequence of events.

---

# Naming

The filename and chapter title share the same title.

Titles describe the day's central discovery rather than its busiest activity.

Examples:

- Quiet Foundations
- Lady Blue Comes Home
- Belonging
- First Invitation
- Homeward
- Resolution

Avoid titles that merely describe tasks, locations, or schedules.

---

# Guiding Principles

If the Operational Record disappeared, the Chronicle would not be detailed enough to reconstruct the day.

If the Chronicle disappeared, the World would eventually forget why the day deserved to be remembered.

The Operational Record remembers the day.

The Chronicle remembers why the day mattered.
