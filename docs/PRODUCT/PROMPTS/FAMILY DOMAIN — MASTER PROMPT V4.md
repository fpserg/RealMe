# FAMILY DOMAIN — MASTER PROMPT V4
## RealMe Atlas — Controlled Domain Detail

Create a wide **16:9 hand-painted fantasy atlas illustration** of the **Family Domain**.

Use **Stronghold V3 as the canonical visual-style reference**.

Family belongs to the same Household Realm, the same geography and the same imaginary atlas.

The objective is to synthesize three qualities:

1. **Stronghold V3:** painterly atmosphere, geographic depth, warm light and sense of a larger inhabited World;
2. **Family V3:** rich emotional and geographic continuity;
3. **the simpler Family reference:** exceptional mobile readability, strong visual glyph and disciplined information density.

The result must NOT simply average these styles.

The target is:

> **STRONGHOLD V3 PAINTERLY WORLD**
> +
> **FAMILY V3 ATMOSPHERE**
> +
> **SIMPLE MOBILE-GAME INFORMATION DISCIPLINE**

The governing rule is:

**same atlas, closer view — but never a different kind of artwork.**

---

# 1. REALME ATLAS SCALE PRINCIPLE

RealMe uses one continuous visual language across:

WORLD MAP → REALM MAP → DOMAIN VIEW

Scale changes by changing **detail**, not style.

WORLD:
large geographic masses and simple landmarks.

REALM:
recognizable Domains and major geographic connections.

DOMAIN:
the same painted geography viewed closer, revealing moderate signs of lived reality.

This is a DOMAIN VIEW.

It should therefore reveal more than a Realm map, but remain fundamentally cartographic.

Do NOT transform the image into:

- cinematic concept art;
- architectural visualization;
- detailed village simulation;
- photorealistic landscape;
- dense fantasy illustration.

The viewer should feel:

> I moved closer to the same map.

---

# 2. DOMAIN DETAIL TARGET — CRITICAL

Use approximately the same effective level of detail as **Stronghold V3**.

Family must NOT become significantly more detailed merely because houses, gardens and fields permit additional objects.

Compared with the previous rich Family generation:

**reduce peripheral information by approximately 25–30%.**

Do this primarily by:

- merging vegetation into masses;
- simplifying distant buildings;
- reducing agricultural subdivisions;
- reducing secondary roads;
- simplifying shorelines;
- removing unnecessary tiny objects;
- allowing atmospheric areas to remain unresolved.

Do NOT simplify the central Family landmark excessively.

The goal is controlled detail, not minimalism.

---

# 3. THE THREE-FORM GLYPH — ABSOLUTE PRIORITY

Family has one permanent visual identity:

# OLD TREE
+
# GATHERED HOMES
+
# WATER

Everything else is supporting geography.

These three forms must remain recognizable at:

- Domain scale;
- Realm scale;
- World scale.

If heavily blurred, the image should still clearly show:

1. one dominant green tree mass;
2. one compact warm-brown cluster of roofs;
3. one adjacent blue-grey body of water.

This is the Family glyph.

Protect it from visual competition.

---

# 4. COMPOSITION

Use the same **high three-quarter aerial perspective** as Stronghold V3.

The image should show substantial surrounding geography and atmospheric depth.

Place the Family settlement almost exactly at the **horizontal centre** of the canvas.

The settlement should occupy approximately **20–25% of the image area**.

The old tree stands at its centre.

Arrange approximately **6–8 principal homes** around it.

Bring these houses relatively close together so that they form one coherent settlement mass when viewed from a distance.

The settlement must NOT become a perfect ring.

The overall composition should read:

                    DISTANT HOUSEHOLD LAND
                           gentle hills

                              ↓

                         LARGE OLD TREE

                    ▰   ▰       ▰
                  ▰       ○       ▰
                    ▰           ▰

                         WATER

                              ↓

                    WIDER GREEN TERRITORY

The composition should be immediately understandable before individual objects are inspected.

---

# 5. THE OLD TREE — PRIMARY LANDMARK

At the centre stands one very old mature deciduous tree.

It is Family's strongest individual landmark.

Give it:

- a broad canopy;
- thick trunk;
- irregular branching;
- asymmetrical natural silhouette;
- visible age;
- healthy but imperfect form.

Its crown should read primarily as several large painterly foliage masses.

Do NOT paint thousands of individual leaves.

Use visible brushwork.

The tree should be large enough to dominate the settlement without becoming fantastically enormous.

It is not magical.

No glow.
No runes.
No sacred architecture.
No mystical effects.

It matters simply because life accumulated around it.

---

# 6. THE HOMES

Place approximately 6–8 principal homes around the tree.

They should feel:

- domestic;
- substantial;
- warm;
- established;
- individually evolved;
- slightly imperfect.

Use restrained combinations of:

- timber;
- warm brick;
- muted plaster;
- stone;
- dark roofs;
- muted red-brown roofs.

Architecture should be simplified in the same painterly manner as Stronghold V3.

At first glance, each building should mostly read as:

**ROOF SHAPE + WALL MASS + OCCASIONAL WARM WINDOW**

Do not showcase individual architecture.

Avoid excessive:

- dormers;
- façade ornament;
- roof tiles;
- window frames;
- timber patterns;
- chimneys.

Individual houses matter less than the collective cluster.

---

# 7. COMPACT BUT ORGANIC SETTLEMENT

The settlement should achieve both:

**MOBILE READABILITY**
and
**NATURAL ACCUMULATION.**

Homes should therefore be relatively compactly grouped around the old tree, but never arranged in a perfect radial pattern.

Some houses sit closer.

Others sit farther away.

Orientation varies.

Extensions differ.

Gardens are irregular.

Paths bend naturally.

The settlement should look as though it accumulated over generations.

But irregularity must remain subordinate to compositional clarity.

Do not confuse realism with visual disorder.

---

# 8. SHARED GREEN

Leave breathing room immediately around the tree.

Create a modest shared green containing only a few understated elements:

- grass;
- several benches;
- perhaps one communal table;
- simple well;
- worn paths.

This area must remain visually quiet.

Negative space around the tree is important.

Do not turn the shared green into:

- marketplace;
- ceremonial plaza;
- park;
- festival ground;
- symbolic monument.

---

# 9. WATER — SECOND MAJOR FORM

Place one large, simple body of water immediately adjacent to the settlement.

Prefer:

**a broad river bend or river widening into a small lake-like area.**

Water should form one coherent blue-grey geographic shape.

Its silhouette matters more than shoreline detail.

The settlement should unmistakably read as:

> FAMILY BESIDE WATER.

The water may continue through the landscape toward the distance, helping create geographic depth.

Allow only a few secondary features:

- one simple wooden bridge;
- one modest jetty;
- perhaps one or two tiny boats;
- restrained reeds.

Do not decorate the entire shoreline.

Do not render excessive reflections.

---

# 10. GEOGRAPHIC DEPTH — PRESERVE FROM OUR V3

Unlike an isolated board-game tile, the Domain must clearly belong to a much larger World.

Beyond the central settlement, show Household territory continuing toward the horizon.

Use:

- rolling green terrain;
- distant hills;
- broad woodland masses;
- meadows;
- several cultivated areas;
- continuation of river;
- faint settlement marks;
- atmospheric distance.

The viewer should feel that roads and water continue somewhere beyond the visible Domain.

There should be places one could imagine travelling toward.

This sense of geographic continuation is essential to the RealMe Atlas.

However, the distant World must never compete with the central Family glyph.

---

Organize information density into three explicit zones.

## ZONE B — SUPPORTING GEOGRAPHY

Medium-low detail.

## ZONE C — WIDER WORLD
### outer approximately 40%

Low detail.

Do not individually resolve objects.

The distance should increasingly dissolve into atmosphere.

---

# 12. LANDSCAPE INFORMATION BUDGET

The central settlement is allowed complexity.

The surrounding landscape is not.

Use approximately:

- 30% visual information — Family settlement;
- 20% — water and principal connections;
- 15% — cultivated land;
- 15% — woodland;
- 20% — atmospheric/open geography.

Do NOT fill unused visual space.

Empty meadow is useful.

Calm water is useful.

Uninterrupted woodland is useful.

Atmospheric distance is useful.

Visual silence is part of the composition.

---

# 13. PATHS

Use a very limited hierarchy of paths.

Approximately:

**2–3 principal routes**
+
**a few local paths near the settlement.**

Principal routes should visually connect:

FAMILY → WATER

FAMILY → CULTIVATED LAND

FAMILY → WIDER HOUSEHOLD

Do not draw a dense road network.

Do not use roads merely to fill landscape.

At Realm-map reduction, only the principal routes should remain perceptible.

---

# 14. CULTIVATED LAND

Show that generations have shaped the surrounding land.

Include only several broad areas suggesting:

- fields;
- meadow;
- orchard;
- grazing;
- gardens.

Represent these primarily as **large painterly colour masses**.

Use muted variations of:

- green;
- ochre;
- subdued gold;
- earthy brown.

Do not render every crop row.

Do not outline every agricultural parcel.

Do not divide the valley into dozens of tiny fields.

Agriculture should explain inhabitation, not become visual texture.

---

# 15. WOODLAND

Treat forests as geographic forms.

Close to the settlement, several individual trees may be visible.

Farther away, vegetation progressively merges into:

- broad forest silhouettes;
- dark-green masses;
- loose brush textures.

Do NOT individually paint hundreds of trees.

The forest should remain readable when substantially reduced.

---

# 16. SIGNS OF LIFE — DOMAIN-LEVEL REWARD

The Domain view should reward entering Family with a small amount of lived detail absent from Realm scale.

Include only a restrained selection:

- thin chimney smoke;
- several warm windows;
- perhaps 3–5 tiny figures;
- one cart;
- laundry;
- stacked firewood;
- one or two animals;
- one small boat;
- subtle garden activity.

These details should be discovered only after looking closer.

At Realm scale, they disappear naturally.

This is precisely what Domain-level detail is for.

---

# 17. GENERATIONAL ACCUMULATION

Suggest continuity through a few environmental differences:

- mature orchard beside younger trees;
- older house with newer extension;
- worn footpath;
- old low wall reused within a garden;
- different ages of buildings;
- established vegetation beside recently planted areas.

Use restraint.

Do not explain history through symbols.

No ancestor monuments.
No memorials.
No standing stones.
No lineage iconography.

Time should be visible because different things were built at different times.

**one irregular dark-green mass.**

**one broad green brush area.**

**one ochre shape with minimal texture.**

- Family identity;
- geographic understanding;
- atmosphere;
