# FAMILY DOMAIN — MASTER PROMPT V3
## Unified RealMe Atlas Style

Create a wide 16:9 hand-painted fantasy campaign-map illustration of the **Family Domain**.

This image belongs to the same World, Household Realm and visual atlas as **Stronghold V3**.

The objective is NOT to create the most detailed or beautiful fantasy landscape possible.

The objective is to establish a coherent **RealMe Atlas visual language** that can later scale naturally across:

WORLD MAP → REALM MAP → DOMAIN VIEW

All three levels should look as though they were painted by the same atlas artist using the same visual grammar.

Only the level of detail changes.

At Domain level, the viewer has moved closer to the landscape, so more signs of lived reality become visible — but the underlying geography must remain simple, readable and iconic.

The governing principle is:

**PAINTERLY ATMOSPHERE + MOBILE-GAME READABILITY + CARTOGRAPHIC ABSTRACTION**

The image should remain beautiful because of composition, light, shape and atmosphere — NOT because of micro-detail.

---

# 1. SCALE PRINCIPLE — CRITICAL

Imagine one fantasy atlas painted at three viewing distances.

WORLD MAP:
large landmasses, major geography, Domain landmarks as simple visual glyphs.

REALM MAP:
individual Domains recognizable, major roads/rivers/terrain visible, moderate detail.

DOMAIN VIEW:
the same visual language viewed closer, revealing homes, gardens, paths and signs of life.

This image is the DOMAIN level.

Therefore it may contain more information than a Realm map, but it must NEVER become:

- cinematic concept art;
- architectural visualization;
- detailed simulation;
- photorealistic landscape;
- dense Where's-Waldo illustration.

The viewer should always feel:

**I have moved closer to the same painted map.**

Not:

**I have switched from a map into a different kind of artwork.**

---

# 2. INFORMATION-DENSITY BUDGET

Use deliberate restraint.

Approximately:

- 25% of visual information: central Family landmark;
- 20%: adjacent water and principal paths;
- 20%: cultivated landscape;
- 15%: woodland;
- 20%: open atmospheric space and distant geography.

Do not attempt to fill empty areas with additional objects.

Open space is intentional.

The image should contain significantly LESS micro-detail than the previous Family generation.

Reduce peripheral environmental information by approximately **30–40%**.

Use broad painted masses rather than collections of individual objects.

---

# 3. CORE VISUAL GLYPH

Everything is organized around three immediately readable forms:

**OLD TREE**
+
**GATHERED HOMES**
+
**WATER**

These three elements define Family at every zoom level.

At Domain level they receive moderate detail.

At Realm level they will later become simplified.

At World level they may become little more than a tree silhouette, roof cluster and patch of blue water.

Therefore their major shapes must already be extremely strong here.

If the Domain cannot be recognized after blurring the image heavily, the composition is too complicated.

---

# 4. COMPOSITION

Use a high three-quarter aerial perspective consistent with Stronghold V3 and traditional illustrated fantasy campaign maps.

The central Family settlement must sit almost exactly in the **centre of the composition**.

The old tree forms the primary central anchor.

Around it place approximately **6–8 principal family homes**.

IMPORTANT CHANGE FROM V2:

Bring the homes approximately **15–20% closer together**.

They should form a coherent cluster around the old tree without becoming a perfect circle.

The settlement should feel naturally accumulated but visually compact.

Its overall silhouette should survive substantial reduction.

The central composition should read approximately as:

                    DISTANT LAND

                         🌳
                    ▰   ▰   ▰
                  ▰           ▰
                     ▰     ▰

                       ~~~
                    ~~~~~~~~~

                WIDER LANDSCAPE

The water should sit immediately adjacent to the settlement and remain clearly associated with it.

---

# 5. THE OLD TREE

Place one mature deciduous tree at the approximate visual centre.

It must have:

- broad recognizable crown;
- thick irregular trunk;
- strong asymmetrical branching;
- considerable apparent age;
- healthy but imperfect shape.

Make the canopy a single strong painterly mass.

Do NOT individually render hundreds of leaves.

Use visible brushwork and broad clusters of foliage.

The tree should remain unmistakable when the image is viewed very small.

It is NOT:

- magical;
- glowing;
- sacred;
- monumental;
- mystical.

It is simply very old.

Its meaning comes from the fact that life accumulated around it.

---

# 6. THE FAMILY HOMES

Arrange 6–8 principal homes loosely around the old tree.

Architecture should remain recognizable but simplified.

Use:

- timber;
- warm brick;
- muted plaster;
- stone foundations;
- dark or red-brown roofs;
- occasional porch, balcony or terrace.

Homes should vary slightly in age, size and form.

But simplify their surfaces.

Do NOT render:

- individual roof tiles;
- elaborate window frames;
- tiny façade ornament;
- excessive timber patterns;
- complex dormers;
- dozens of chimneys.

At first glance each home should primarily read as:

**ROOF SHAPE + WALL MASS + WARM WINDOW ACCENT**

The houses are part of the Family glyph.

They are not individual architectural showcases.

---

# 7. NATURAL ACCUMULATION

Avoid Gemini-like perfect radial geometry.

The homes did not appear according to a master plan.

Some are closer to the tree.

Others sit slightly farther away.

Paths curve imperfectly between them.

Gardens have different shapes.

One home may have a newer extension.

Another may be older.

But this irregularity must exist **inside an overall readable composition**.

The desired balance is:

**STRUCTURALLY CLEAR**
but
**HUMANLY IMPERFECT**

Do not sacrifice readability in pursuit of realism.

---

# 8. SHARED GREEN

Around the tree create a simple open green.

Keep it visually quiet.

Possible elements:

- grass;
- several benches;
- one communal table;
- a simple well;
- worn paths.

Do not fill it with objects.

The old tree should have visual breathing room around its trunk.

This negative space is important for mobile readability.

---

# 9. WATER

Water is the second major geographic form.

Place a broad river bend, river widening or small lake immediately beside the settlement.

Its geometry should be simple and unmistakable.

Prefer **one broad blue-grey shape** rather than multiple complicated streams.

A river may continue away from it into the wider landscape.

Include only restrained secondary details:

- one simple bridge;
- small wooden jetty;
- perhaps 1–2 boats;
- reeds suggested with brushstrokes.

Do not over-render the shoreline.

Do not cover the water with reflections and tiny ripples.

At reduced scale it should simply remain:

**FAMILY SETTLEMENT BESIDE WATER.**

---

# 10. PRINCIPAL PATHS

Simplify the road network substantially compared with previous generations.

Use:

- 2–3 principal paths;
- only a few secondary footpaths.

The strongest routes connect:

TREE / HOMES → WATER

TREE / HOMES → CULTIVATED LAND

TREE / HOMES → WIDER HOUSEHOLD REALM

Do not create a dense web of roads.

Paths should function as large compositional gestures.

---

# 11. CULTIVATED LAND

Show that generations have shaped the surrounding territory.

Use broad painterly patches representing:

- several fields;
- meadow;
- orchard;
- grazing land;
- gardens.

IMPORTANT:

Fields should read primarily as **large shapes of subtly different green, ochre and muted gold**.

Do not draw every crop row.

Do not outline every field.

Do not create dozens of agricultural parcels.

One orchard may be represented by several grouped tree marks rather than individually detailed trees.

Agriculture is part of the geography.

It is not the subject.

---

# 12. WOODLAND

Represent woodland mostly as broad masses.

Near the settlement, a few individual trees may be visible.

Farther away, trees merge progressively into painted forest shapes.

Avoid rendering hundreds of separate trees.

Use differences in:

- value;
- silhouette;
- brush texture;
- colour temperature

to imply forest depth.

This is essential for maintaining the same visual abstraction that will later appear on Realm and World maps.

---

# 13. DISTANT LANDSCAPE

The landscape extends toward gentle hills and distant Household territory.

Use:

- broad hills;
- woodland masses;
- open meadows;
- continuation of water;
- perhaps faint distant settlement marks.

Do not create additional visually important landmarks.

Do not add dramatic mountains.

Stronghold owns the snowy mountain silhouette within Household.

Family should own:

**gentle green land + old tree + homes + water.**

Distant geography should increasingly dissolve into atmospheric brushwork.

---

# 14. DETAIL GRADIENT — CRITICAL

Detail must decrease strongly with distance from the central Family settlement.

Use this hierarchy:

### CENTRAL 25%
Moderate illustrative detail.

Recognizable homes.
Tree structure.
Simple gardens.
Main paths.

### MIDDLE 35%
Simplified geography.

Broad fields.
Grouped woodland.
River.
Occasional building marks.

### OUTER 40%
Very low detail.

Painted terrain masses.
Atmospheric hills.
Forest silhouettes.
Water continuation.

This gradient is essential.

Do NOT apply equal detail across the canvas.

---

# 15. SIGNS OF LIFE

Family must feel inhabited.

Use only a few subtle signs:

- thin chimney smoke;
- several warm windows;
- 2–4 tiny human figures;
- one cart;
- perhaps laundry;
- stacked firewood;
- one or two animals;
- one small boat.

These should reward looking closer.

They must disappear naturally at Realm-map scale.

Do not create crowds.

Do not create busy activity.

---

# 16. GENERATIONAL CONTINUITY

Suggest time through environmental accumulation rather than explicit symbolism.

Possible examples:

- mature orchard beside several younger trees;
- old house with newer extension;
- worn path;
- old low stone wall incorporated into garden;
- newer porch beside older structure;
- mature vegetation beside recently planted areas.

Use very few such signals.

They should be discovered rather than announced.

No ancestor monuments.
No standing stones.
No lineage symbols.
No memorial architecture.

---

# 17. PAINTERLY ABSTRACTION — CRITICAL

The image should be noticeably **sketchier and less resolved** than our previous Family V2.

Use:

- broad brushstrokes;
- simplified vegetation;
- grouped forms;
- slightly loose edges;
- visible painterly construction;
- selective definition;
- restrained linework.

Allow some objects to remain suggested rather than fully described.

A distant cottage can be:

**two brush shapes and a roof.**

A forest can be:

**one irregular dark-green mass with several tree silhouettes.**

A field can be:

**one ochre-green shape with subtle texture.**

Water can be:

**one calm blue-grey painted gesture.**

Do not interpret "beautiful" as "detailed."

---

# 18. MOBILE-GAME READABILITY WITHOUT MOBILE-GAME AESTHETICS

Borrow the information architecture of successful mobile-game environments:

- strong silhouettes;
- clear object separation;
- large readable shapes;
- controlled density;
- immediate hierarchy;
- limited competing focal points.

But do NOT borrow their visual aesthetic.

Avoid:

- cartoonish proportions;
- glossy surfaces;
- saturated game colours;
- toy-like houses;
- exaggerated UI-friendly objects;
- polished 3D appearance;
- asset-pack aesthetics.

The image remains traditional fantasy illustration.

The lesson taken from mobile games is **clarity**, not appearance.

---

# 19. LIGHTING

Match Stronghold V3.

Use warm early-morning or late-afternoon sunlight.

Light should gently touch:

- upper old-tree canopy;
- selected roofs;
- part of the shared green;
- several fields;
- water.

Use cooler subdued values in woodland and shaded terrain.

Maintain atmospheric haze toward the horizon.

Lighting should unify large forms rather than reveal additional texture.

Avoid:

- HDR;
- extreme sunset;
- dramatic god rays;
- glowing fog;
- cinematic contrast.

The scene should feel:

**painted from memory.**

---

# 20. UNIFIED REALME ATLAS STYLE — CRITICAL

This is the central art-direction requirement.

The image should look like one page of a larger hand-painted fantasy atlas.

Stronghold V3, Family V3, future Capital Domain, Household Realm and eventually the World Map should all plausibly be paintings by the SAME ARTIST.

The style consists of:

- high three-quarter aerial perspective;
- hand-painted fantasy cartography;
- broad geographic forms;
- readable landmark silhouettes;
- warm restrained atmospheric lighting;
- moderately muted natural colours;
- visible brushwork;
- selective rather than exhaustive detail;
- slightly sketchy architecture;
- atmospheric depth;
- gentle idealization of geography.

As scale increases from Domain → Realm → World:

**do not change style.**

Instead:

**remove detail.**

This principle is absolute.

---

# 21. MOBILE-SAFE COMPOSITION

Master artwork: **16:9 landscape**.

RealMe is primarily viewed through a narrow central phone viewport.

The central portrait-shaped safe area must contain:

- entire old tree;
- entire shared green;
- nearly all principal Family homes;
- clear portion of adjacent water;
- at least one principal path;
- enough surrounding land to establish context.

The Family glyph must therefore sit almost exactly around the horizontal centre.

Do not place important houses near extreme left or right edges.

Outer horizontal areas contain expendable:

- fields;
- forest;
- distant hills;
- continuation of water.

Aggressive cropping from BOTH sides must still leave a complete Family composition.

---

# 22. BLUR TEST

Mentally blur the image until small details disappear.

The viewer must still perceive:

1. one large green tree mass;
2. one compact warm-brown roof cluster;
3. one adjacent blue-grey water mass;
4. surrounding green territory.

If the identity disappears under blur:

SIMPLIFY.

If another landscape feature becomes equally prominent:

SIMPLIFY.

If individual houses compete with the old tree:

SIMPLIFY.

# 25. EMOTIONAL CHARACTER

Family should feel:

- familiar;
- warm;
- rooted;
- safe;
- connected;
- imperfect;
- generational;
- quietly alive.

But avoid sentimentality.

Do not make everything golden and idyllic.

Do not make every garden perfect.

Do not make every home picturesque.

The place has meaning because people live there.

Not because the landscape has been designed to symbolize Family.

---

# 26. FINAL FEELING

The image should communicate implicitly:

A tree stood here.

Homes accumulated nearby.

Paths formed between them.

People shaped the surrounding land.

Water became familiar.

Different generations changed different parts of the place.

No one designed the complete settlement.

It became Family.

The viewer should understand this emotionally without needing any literal symbolism.

Most importantly:

**this should look like a closer view of the same World Map we will eventually build — not a separate illustration inspired by it.**
